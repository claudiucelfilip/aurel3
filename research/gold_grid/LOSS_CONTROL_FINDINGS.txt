Gold-grid loss-control experiments — 2026-10-21
================================================

Verified on this reconstruction:
- All eight original baseline summaries reproduce with the controls disabled.
- 22 targeted baseline/risk tests pass.
- Independent accounting reconciles 2,244,844 fills in 144 main comparisons;
  maximum cash discrepancy is $0.000000007956.
- 72 loss-control comparisons: nine named rules, two sizing modes, two starting
  years and both assumed minute paths. 54 nearby settings and 16 cost stresses
  are also reported. Profit locks add 72 comparisons in an adaptive second round.
- No named rule or profit-lock setting beat SPY in BOTH periods and BOTH paths.

Selected settings were declared before their own results, not chosen as winners:
- Combined control: maximum four purchases, new-purchase notional at most 2x
  basket-start cash, exit at 2% loss after costs, pause 24 hours after a loss.
- Profit lock: original grid; activate a 20% trailing account exit after the
  net-equity peak first reaches 2x initial capital. Permanently stop afterward.

Fixed-size purchases, $10,000 initial capital, low-first path:
                              2019 onward       Fresh 2022 onward
Original grid                    $6,778               $6,396
Combined 2% control              $2,479               $1,973
Profit lock                     $17,276              $24,480
SPY                             $34,184              $17,024
Unleveraged spot gold            $32,386              $22,706

Why the profitable 2022 profit-lock result is insufficient:
- Its worst drawdown was 83.0% BEFORE the delayed protection activated.
- The same setting's 2019 account lost 64.4% from a peak before activation,
  and ended far below SPY after stopping in October 2025.
- A trailing stop can exit before the later peak and miss the recovery.
- Its nominal 20% trail is not a 20% maximum drawdown over the account's life.

Other observations:
- An account-wide 20% exit active from inception halted much earlier and preserved
  roughly $8,000-$9,000 in the default combined runs; it never reached the later peak.
- All 54 combined-control sensitivity settings lost money in the full 2019
  low-first sample. Counting small losses and costs is essential.
- Exposure/entry caps alone avoided margin liquidation in the main runs but
  sometimes left large losing baskets open at the sample end. Those open
  losses are fully included in equity, not discarded as unfinished trades.
- Some stopped accounts could no longer afford the fixed one-ounce first order
  within their risk budget. This is reported separately from margin liquidation.

Assumed and limited:
- Same declared original settings, synthetic minute paths, quote filtering,
  financing and margin policy as the baseline; no recovered creator source code.
- First-round candidates were frozen before their results, but after the full
  baseline history had been inspected. Profit locks were designed after that
  round. Neither is independent out-of-sample validation.
- Gaps/funding can exceed nominal loss limits. No losses or balances are clamped.
- No cash replenishment, hidden restart, interest on idle cash or personal tax.
- No AI filter, model training, new strategy selection or live deployment.

Conclusion:
These experiments do not justify replacing SPY with this gold grid. Some rules
preserve profits or reduce a single crash, but the tested rules did not combine
competitive full-period returns with acceptable drawdowns across start dates.
This is evidence about the tested reconstruction, not every possible grid.

Reproduce with loss_controls.py, profit_locks.py and loss_report.py, following
README.txt. Full snapshots are loss_control_snapshot.json and profit_lock_snapshot.json.
